Frequently asked questions

The fifteen things people ask most about ClearDeck: why it uses a VPN, whether it needs root, what it collects, how many rules ship inside it, and how “traffic saved” is actually calculated.

  • Android 10 – 17
  • v1.0.0
  • 446,709 rules

  1. Is this a VPN? Why does it need a VPN connection?

    ClearDeck uses Android’s VpnService for one reason: it is the only way an ordinary, non-root app can see every other app’s traffic on the device. The VPN interface is a local tunnel — traffic is inspected on your phone and then goes straight out. There is no remote node and nothing is relayed.

  2. Does my traffic go through your servers?

    No. Nothing leaves the device. Connections are judged against the rule lists on your phone: a match is rejected immediately, and everything else goes straight out over your normal connection. There is no remote node and no server that relays or stores your traffic.

  3. Do I need root?

    No. ClearDeck runs as a normal app and asks only for Android’s standard VPN authorization. No root, no modified system files, no unlocked bootloader — so OTA updates are unaffected. Some banking or security apps may simply note that a VPN is active; that is their own detection policy, not a root check.

  4. Does it work on my phone?

    It supports Android 10 – 17 (minSdk 29), on arm64 devices only. The app ships just the arm64-v8a ABI, so x86_64 emulators cannot run it.

  5. Is it free? Any ads or subscriptions?

    The core app is free and carries no advertising. Ad blocking, deep ad blocking and malicious site blocking are membership modules: the Huawei build uses Huawei AppGallery payment, the OPPO build uses Alipay and the Xiaomi build uses the Xiaomi app store (app joint operation); the Google build includes no membership purchase. The first time you enable any restricted module you get a 14-day trial. No build requires a ClearDeck account.

  6. What exactly gets collected?

    Only the Huawei and Xiaomi builds include analytics: device and usage statistics and crash reports, handled by the Umeng SDK. The Google and OPPO builds contain no analytics code at all — not one line of Umeng code, not one related permission or manifest entry. Network traffic content is never collected.

  7. Why does the app ask me to accept a privacy notice before it will run?

    Consent is a hard gate by design: the notice appears on first launch, and if you decline it confirms once and then the app will not run. The gate ensures that any component which might process data starts only after you agree, most notably the analytics SDKs in the Huawei and Xiaomi builds. Either way, your traffic never leaves the device. To check for rule updates, the app requests a rule manifest before consent is given; that request carries no device identifier and no account.

  8. How many rules are included, and does it need to download them?

    Five built-in function modules totalling 446,709 entries, about 9.8 MB inside the APK. They ship with the app, so a fresh install works offline without downloading any module package, though it does request a rule manifest once (version numbers and download URLs only; no device identifier or account). Afterwards the app checks for newer lists every 3 days (and you can check by hand from the modules page), downloading only the modules whose version actually moved. Automatic downloads happen only on unmetered networks; a manual check is not restricted that way. It works fine without ever going online. You can optionally import your own file or subscription link, but nothing is required. Defaults: tracker blocking and deep tracker blocking are on; ad blocking, deep ad blocking and malicious site blocking are off, and enabling any of them starts a 14-day trial.

  9. What is “traffic saved” and is it accurate?

    It is an estimate, not a measurement. Each blocked connection is priced at what the same hostname delivered per allowed connection; a host that was only ever blocked falls back to a fixed 16 KiB. That means it can over- or under-state the real figure — treat it as a rough idea of what you avoided, not an exact count.

  10. Will it slow down my phone or drain the battery?

    Measured on a OnePlus 6T over 30 minutes, the tunnel process used 20.67 seconds of CPU — about 1.148% of one core. The UI process used only 19 jiffies in the same window because Android freezes it in the background. A power saving mode widens the sampling intervals and pauses deep inspection, at the cost of recording fewer short-lived connections.

  11. What is deep inspection and do I need it?

    Deep inspection decrypts a connection on your phone so the app can match the full URL path, not just the hostname. It needs a ClearDeck certificate installed, and it only helps the deep URL rules. Many people do not need it — domain rules alone block most things — and it does nothing until you turn it on and install the certificate. Content over the inspection limit (default 256 KB) is passed through unchecked.

  12. Can I block or allow a single app?

    Yes. In an app’s access log every record has Block and Allow on the right; tapping either opens an “Apply to…” chooser where you can scope the rule to that app only or to all apps. What you add collects in My block & allow on the modules screen.

  13. Where do the rule lists come from?

    Ad rules come from EasyList, EasyList Ad Servers, 217heidai/adblockfilters and AWAvenue Ads Rule; tracker rules from EasyPrivacy, AdGuard Tracking Protection and AWAvenue Ads Rule; malware rules from Hagezi Threat Intelligence Feeds (TIF, mini). The lists ship with the app for offline use, and the sources are listed inside the app.

  14. Can I export my data?

    Yes — the tools screen exports the access log as CSV (RFC 4180, UTF-8 with BOM) with package, app, time, host and result columns. But records are cleared when protection stops, so export before you stop it if you want to keep them. There is no cloud sync and no ClearDeck account.

  15. Where do I get ClearDeck?

    ClearDeck has been released as v1.0.0 and is rolling out to app stores. Search for “ClearDeck” (御流 in the Chinese UI) and check that the developer is Beijing Fenghong Technology Co., Ltd. If it does not appear yet, that store’s rollout is still in progress. There is no self-hosted APK, no F-Droid and no GitHub Releases; each of the four builds is distributed by its own store.
