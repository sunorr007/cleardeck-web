Three common ways to block ads on Android

Each of them costs you something. ClearDeck takes a fourth path: decide locally, in a firewall rule table on the device. No server in the middle, no root.

✕

VPN blockers that relay traffic

Your traffic is routed through someone else’s server to be filtered. You lose the ads, and you hand over your browsing history with them — usually for a monthly fee.

✕

Root-based hosts files

Requires root, edits system files, and can break OTA updates and banking apps. Rooting a modern phone is a losing battle.

✕

Browser extensions

Only cover the browser. Ads inside apps and an app’s own tracking calls are untouched.

ClearDeck

Connections are judged on your phone: a match is rejected on the spot, and everything else goes straight out. No remote node and no traffic relay — judging and forwarding both happen on the phone.

Two layers, each doing its own job

Domain layer

The hostname is often the whole answer

When a connection lands on an ad domain, the hostname alone decides it. Domain rules are plain host lists — nothing is decrypted, and a match is rejected on sight. 435,174 of them ship in the app.

Deep layer

Some ads need the full address

On one site, the article page and the ad slot can share a hostname. Deep inspection decrypts the connection on your phone, reads the full path, and judges that. /article is allowed; /promo is not. 11,535 URL rules, which do nothing until you switch deep inspection on.
