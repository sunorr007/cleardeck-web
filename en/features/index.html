Features

ClearDeck does a handful of things, and each one earns its place. This page walks through them in the order the app presents them.

  • 428,423 bundled rules
  • 5 function modules
  • Android 10 – 17
  • no root

Blocking, in two layers

ClearDeck ships five rule modules inside the app, so they work offline from the moment you install, and they keep themselves up to date. Three of them are domain lists: the connection names a host, the host is on the list, and the connection is rejected on the phone before it leaves.

The other two are deep-layer lists. They only take effect once deep inspection is on, because judging them means reading the full address rather than just the hostname.

Module Layer Entries
Ad blocking Domain 92,232
Tracker blocking Domain 135,812
Deep ad blocking Deep 1,938
Deep tracker blocking Deep 9,659
Malicious site blocking Domain 188,782

428,423 entries in total, about 9.5 MB inside the installed app.

The two deep modules only match once deep inspection is on. Until then their cards show as Paused, with a one-tap way to turn it on.

The three domain modules apply to every app. The two deep modules have their own scope (see Deep inspection below). Turning a module off asks for confirmation first and warns that its domains will no longer be blocked.

See each app’s traffic

Tap the active-connections number on the dashboard and you land on the app list. It has two tabs: Live shows what is connected right now, and History shows how many times an app tried, how many were blocked, and how long ago it last acted.

Open one app and you get Upload / Download / Saved, its blocked categories (deep-inspection hits are marked Deep), and its own access log. Every record in that log has a Block and an Allow button beside it. Either one opens Apply to…, where you choose This app only or All apps — so a single line in the log becomes a rule scoped exactly as wide as you meant.

Live and history

Two tabs: what is connected right now, and what each app has done over time — attempts, blocks, and the last time it acted.

A rule from one line

Block or Allow on any access-log record, then Apply to… — This app only or All apps.

Filters, grouping, export

All / Blocked only / Allowed only; By time or By site; remove a rule you added; Export log from the detail screen.

Your own lists

Two ways in, and both end up as modules you control, sitting alongside the bundled ones.

Import file

Bring your own rule file — domains, IPs or full addresses. ClearDeck shows what it read and what it will do before anything is applied.

Import link

Paste a rule link. Only https:// links are accepted, because a rule list is firewall rules and should not be rewritten in transit. Update refreshes it in one tap and shows when it last updated.

My block & allow

Everything you add by hand collects in one card alongside the modules. It is deliberately not a one-tap off switch. When it is empty it tells you how to fill it: open an app, expand its access log and tap Block or Allow on a record.

Deep inspection

Deep inspection is optional and off until you turn it on. It is the only part of ClearDeck that reads a connection, and that is only possible with a certificate you install on the device: an app’s requests are encrypted, and the certificate is what lets ClearDeck show them to you. The certificate stays on the phone and is never uploaded.

With it on, matching moves from the hostname to the URL path, so an article and an ad slot on the same host can be told apart. The screen carries a three-state badge — On / Off / Paused — and if the certificate is missing it says so plainly: “Not working yet: the ClearDeck certificate is not installed.”

Scope
All apps, or Only the apps I pick — with search and a Selected only filter.
Wi-Fi only
On mobile data, deep inspection turns itself off.
Content inspection limit
128 KB / 256 KB / 512 KB / 1 MB, default 256 KB. Anything above the limit passes straight through; a larger limit inspects more and uses more memory, a smaller one saves power.
Certificate
Install certificate writes the certificate to Downloads and opens the system certificate settings; remove it under Trusted credentials → User.

Statistics

The dashboard leads with four numbers, switchable across Today / 7 days / 30 days:

Connections active right now — tap it to reach the app list
Traffic upload and download combined
Blocked how many connections were rejected
Traffic saved traffic that never left, because it was blocked
  • Blocked breakdown is a ring split by module, with a Custom rules slice and at most six slices — anything past that merges into Other.
  • Traffic destinations fold by site instead of fragmenting into dozens of subdomains.
  • Top 5 apps ranks by usage, with More opening the full history.
  • Two more read-outs sit at the bottom: the DNS service currently in effect, and the intranet IP.

Everyday operation

The things you set once and then stop thinking about.

Start with system
Start with system jumps to Android’s VPN list; open ClearDeck’s settings there and switch on Always-on VPN. After that it comes up at boot with no tap.
Encrypted DNS
Domain lookups travel over an encrypted connection, with a server you can swap at any time.
Access log window
How many records to keep: 100 / 500 / 1000 / 2000, default 500. A bigger window keeps more records and costs more memory.
Power saving
One switch. It slows the speed read-out and pauses deep inspection to stretch the battery; while it is on, deep blocks and recent-activity records become fewer.
export

Export access log

Tools does one job: export the access log as CSV, to keep or analyse yourself. Records are cleared when protection stops, so export first if you want to keep them.

Measured: 30 minutes of protection costs about 1.148% of one core (on a OnePlus 6T — a measurement, not a promise).

Requirements

What you need — and what you do not.

System
Android 10 – 17.
Processor
arm64-v8a devices only.
Root
Not needed. ClearDeck uses Android’s own VPN interface.
Account
No ClearDeck account is needed.
Ads
No advertising inside the app.
what it will not do There is no remote node and no server that relays or stores your traffic. No account is required, and your traffic does not leave the phone.
