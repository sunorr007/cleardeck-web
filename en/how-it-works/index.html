How it works

A connection arrives, a rule decides, and the result lands in the access log. All of it happens on your phone.

Why it needs a VPN connection

On Android, an app has exactly one supported way to see where other apps connect: the system VPN interface. ClearDeck asks for it and uses it as a local firewall.

Once it is on, each app’s connections pass through ClearDeck before they are allowed or refused. That is the one VPN prompt Android shows — your traffic is not handed to any server.

The important part is that nothing is forwarded. ClearDeck has no server that relays traffic and none that stores your browsing. The middle machine that the usual "VPN ad blocker" relies on simply is not there.

Two layers, because a hostname is not always enough

Some requests give themselves away by domain; some need the full address.

Domain layer

The hostname is the answer

A connection to ad.doubleclick.net announces itself. A match is rejected on sight, with nothing decrypted. 416,826 names ship with the app.

Cost: essentially none. Negligible CPU, and no request content is ever seen.

Deep layer

One site, many pages

On one site, the article page and the ad slot often share a domain and differ only by path. Deep inspection reads the whole address before deciding, so /article is allowed and /promo is not. 11,597 address rules, active once deep inspection is on.

Cost: this layer has to decrypt, so it is off by default and can be limited to the apps you choose.

Every connection maps to an app

ClearDeck works out which app each connection belongs to, which is why every number on the dashboard can be split by app.

A connection it cannot attribute is neither dropped nor miscounted: it shows up as an unknown source.

Where these numbers come from

Blocked count

every rejected connection is counted once, without waiting for a refresh — so nothing is missed.

Traffic destinations

folded by site, so subdomains of one site become one row instead of dozens.

Traffic saved

an estimate: priced against what the same site averaged on allowed connections; a target that was only ever blocked is charged a flat 16 KiB per block. It can read high or low — treat it as a guide.

this is an estimate Traffic saved is an estimate, not a bill.

What it deliberately does not do

  • No remote node, and no server that relays or stores your traffic.
  • No root, no hosts file, no system changes.
  • It does not keep your phone awake: protection lives on a foreground service, not on a wake lock.
  • It does not collect the content of your traffic. Ever.

What it costs

Thirty minutes of protection costs about 1.148% of one core.

Power saving lowers the refresh rate and pauses deep inspection for less drain; the price is coarser records for very short connections. Blocked connections are unaffected.
